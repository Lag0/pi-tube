# Paid transcription

On 2026-11-24, the [audit](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/issues/31) and [Lag0/pi-tube@ea99bcb](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/blob/ea99bcb/src/core/application/transcribe.ts) showed why settling a collision when saving a transcript was too late: two ordinary callers could already have dispatched paid work. The [cleanup spec’s concurrent-use criterion](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/issues/32) protected files from deletion, not callers from a second charge. See [Cleanup and portability](cleanup.md) for the scope of the first delivery.

On 2026-11-24, [spec #38](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/issues/38) and [ticket #39](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/issues/39) scoped the first fix to overlapping ordinary requests. One fake provider invocation across independent CLI/MCP processes is stronger evidence than one saved file. Bare `force` expresses a new intent; a provider accepting work before a process crashes leaves a different, unresolved billing question. No exactly-once recovery guarantee was made.

**Delivery update, 2026-11-24 (supersedes proposal-only status):** [Lag0/pi-tube@0d8c922](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/commit/0d8c922) merged [PR #41](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/pull/41). A CLI process and a separate stdio MCP process sharing an ordinary cache miss made one fake provider call. The [CI run on the reviewed head](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/actions/runs/45479197723) passed on Windows, Ubuntu and macOS. Regression tests also distinguished a pathname appearing from publication finishing, and a busy automatic-language cache reader from a miss. The merge closed #39; #38 was then closed for its ordinary-overlap scope. Neither closure settled forced-retry identity or recovery after an uncertain charge.

On 2026-11-24, [spec #47](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/issues/47) and [ticket #48](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/issues/48) distinguished a failed Markdown write after a paid JSON save from a failed transcription. The specified recovery used the same ordinary request, not `--force`; the test budget was one fake dispatch across the initial failure, persistent repair failure and eventual successful repair.

On 2026-11-24, review of [Lag0/pi-tube@f4ba1fe](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/commit/f4ba1fe) showed why waiting for one copy to settle did not certify its successor. The [council decision on #49](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/pull/49#issuecomment-7145441017) called for a distinct token for each fallback publication, recorded as pending before exposure and committed after success. This was a design decision, not a merge or a crash-recovery guarantee.

**Delivery update, 2026-11-24 (supersedes design-only status):** [Lag0/pi-tube@d753e48](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/commit/d753e48) merged [PR #49](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/pull/49). The [CI run on reviewed head `805cfa3`](https://github.com/Lag0/pi-tube/actions/runs/45500870734) passed on Windows, Ubuntu and macOS. The temporary-library test observed one fake provider call across failed Markdown publication, failed repair and successful ordinary repair, with unchanged canonical JSON bytes and ID. Two ordinary calls encountering a reserved but absent JSON made zero fake provider calls. #47 and #48 were closed; these tests did not establish recovery after arbitrary crashes or external changes.
